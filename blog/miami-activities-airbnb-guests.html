10 Miami activities your Airbnb guests will actually book

Guests ask the same question in almost every Miami stay: "What should we do while we're here?" Here are the ten activities that come up most, who each one fits, and how to recommend them so guests actually book.

📅 Published October 19, 2026 ✏️ By Refstay 🏷️ Activities

The 10 activities

  1. Jet ski to the Haulover Sandbar
  2. Biscayne Bay boat tour
  3. Everglades airboat ride
  4. Sunset cruise
  5. Private boat or yacht charter
  6. Kayak or paddleboard at Oleta River State Park
  7. Polaris Slingshot rental
  8. Parasailing in Miami Beach
  9. Snorkel or dive trip
  10. Wynwood art or food walking tour
  11. How to recommend activities so guests book

The best recommendation isn't the most famous activity. It's the one that matches the guest: a couple on an anniversary, a bachelorette group, a family with kids or a solo traveler. Match the activity to the guest and send it at the right moment, and the booking rate goes up on its own.

#1

Jet ski to the Haulover Sandbar

Best for: Couples, friend groups, bachelor and bachelorette weekends

Jet skis are one of the most-booked water activities in Miami, and the north end of Biscayne Bay has the best payoff: the Haulover Sandbar, Raccoon Island and the Oleta River State Park shoreline are all close together. Two people can share one jet ski, so it's an easy yes for couples.

Host tip: If your listing is in Sunny Isles, Aventura, Bal Harbour or North Miami Beach, recommend a north-bay launch instead of sending guests down to South Beach. One example is Jet Ski Rental Miami Beach, which rides to the Haulover Sandbar from $160 per hour. Remind guests born in 1988 or later that Florida requires boating safety education; a short online test covers visitors.

#2

Biscayne Bay boat tour

Best for: Families, first-time visitors, guests who don't want to drive anything

The classic sightseeing cruise past the downtown skyline, the Port of Miami and the waterfront mansions of the bay islands. Low effort, works for all ages and is easy to fit in on arrival day.

Host tip: Suggest it for the first or second day of a stay: it gives guests a feel for the city and makes them more likely to book a second, more active experience later.

#3

Everglades airboat ride

Best for: Families, international visitors, nature lovers

The day trip almost every international guest asks about. Airboat rides through the sawgrass are often paired with a wildlife show, and the drive from Miami makes it a half-day outing.

Host tip: Recommend a morning slot so guests are back for the beach in the afternoon, and tell them to bring a hat and water.

#4

Sunset cruise

Best for: Couples, anniversaries, birthday dinners

Short, social and photogenic. Sunset cruises are an easy recommendation for guests celebrating something and for the last night of a trip.

Host tip: Send it the day before check-out: "If you haven't done a sunset cruise yet, tomorrow is your last chance."

#5

Private boat or yacht charter

Best for: Groups of 6 or more, celebrations, guests with bigger budgets

Groups split the cost, pick the music and usually ask the captain to stop at a sandbar. For large groups it's often better value per person than separate activities.

Host tip: Ask group bookings about it before arrival. Charters book up on weekends and holidays, so early messages convert best.

#6

Kayak or paddleboard at Oleta River State Park

Best for: Active guests, couples, budget-conscious travelers

Florida's largest urban park has mangrove trails and calm water on Biscayne Bay. It's quieter and cheaper than motorized options and a nice morning activity.

Host tip: Great for guests who already did a boat day and want something slower.

#7

Polaris Slingshot rental

Best for: Couples, car lovers, content creators

Open-air three-wheelers that turn a drive down Ocean Drive or across the causeways into the activity itself. In Florida a regular driver's license is enough.

Host tip: Mention the age minimum set by the rental company and that it's open-air, so guests should plan around rain.

#8

Parasailing in Miami Beach

Best for: Thrill-seekers, couples, teens with parents

A quick, high-impact activity off Miami Beach with big views of the coastline. Most flights take only part of the boat trip, so it fits into a beach day.

Host tip: Pair it with a beach recommendation near the departure point so the day feels planned.

#9

Snorkel or dive trip

Best for: Ocean lovers, certified divers, families with older kids

Reef trips run from Miami and nearby areas when the weather allows. They sell well to guests who already know they want to be in the water.

Host tip: Point out that trips depend on wind and visibility, so a flexible booking is worth it.

#10

Wynwood art or food walking tour

Best for: Rainy days, culture fans, solo travelers

Your best rainy-day backup. Street-art and food tours are walkable, social and need no swimwear.

Host tip: Keep it in your welcome book as the bad-weather plan; it saves the day when water activities get rescheduled.

How to recommend activities so guests book

  • Match the guest. Use the booking details you already have: group size, occasion and ages.
  • Keep it short. Three tailored picks beat a list of twenty.
  • Send it at the right time. One message before arrival for activities that sell out (charters, weekend jet skis), and one on day two for everything else.
  • Think about location. Guests prefer what's close. Hosts in Sunny Isles and Aventura should lean on north-bay activities; hosts in Brickell and downtown on bay cruises and the Everglades.
  • Always include a rainy-day option. It keeps the trip on track and keeps your review safe.
